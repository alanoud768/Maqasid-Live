Bias in ChatGPT Recommendations of Synthetic Riyadh Tourist Places for Travellers with Visual, Hearing, and Mobility Disabilities, and the Effect of a Rule-Based Accessibility Filter

By Alanoud Alsubaie, Reem Alobaid, Hissah ALAbdulkarim, Rama Alarifi and Jory Alghunaim. Supervised by Dr. Eman Aljabarti.

SW 350 Group 4 · Princess Nourah bint Abdulrahman University

Would you tell an AI travel planner that you use a wheelchair?

Our main finding

Telling ChatGPT about a disability made its picks safer. Sometimes, it also made them narrower.

Why it matters

More travellers now ask ChatGPT where to go. A traveller with a disability has to choose. Mention it, and risk being steered. Or stay quiet, and risk being sent somewhere the traveller cannot use.

We reviewed 26 studies, and none had tested this for travellers with visual, hearing or mobility disabilities.

Our four questions

  1. Does stating the disability change the picks?
  2. Does it steer travellers away from places they could still enjoy?
  3. Does our filter apply its accessibility rules correctly?
  4. Is filtering first better than stating the disability?

How we tested

We created 144 fictional places in Riyadh. Each one lists 23 accessibility facilities, such as step-free entrances, lifts and audio guides.

We wrote 15 rules, based on published studies and guidelines, to decide who can use each place.

Then we asked OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol model to pick 5 places, 2,000 times, for three travellers: a wheelchair user, a blind traveller and a Deaf traveller. Every request was the same, except for one sentence: "I use a wheelchair", "I'm blind", or "I'm Deaf". Every answer was then scored by code.

One real example

A traveller asked for history and heritage places. Without mentioning a disability, ChatGPT picked five places. Four of them could not be used in a wheelchair. Three had steps at the entrance, and one had no step-free path.

When the traveller added "I use a wheelchair", all five places were suitable.

We chose this example because it shows the effect clearly. Across all 100 wheelchair answers, those with an unsuitable place fell from 89 to 33.

Results

Figure 1. Answers with a place the traveller could not use

Stating the disability cut them for all three travellers. For the wheelchair user, from 89 out of 100 to 33. For the blind traveller, from 40 to 1. For the Deaf traveller, from 31 to 3. Still, about 1 in 3 wheelchair answers had a place the traveller could not use.

Figure 2. Places from types linked to the disability

This shows how many of the 5 places came from types that depend on the sense or movement the disability affects, such as museums and viewpoints for the blind traveller.

In the main test, stating the disability led to more of these places, not fewer. In the follow-up tests, it led to fewer. When every place was suitable, they dropped for the wheelchair user and the Deaf traveller. When facility information was hidden, they dropped for all three. For the blind traveller, history museums and observation decks disappeared completely.

Figure 3. How well the filter worked

Our filter applied every rule correctly. It passed 35 out of 35 logic tests and 11 out of 11 data checks. None of the 600 answers given after filtering included an unsuitable place.

Figure 4. The filter running

A screenshot of our filter running on the study data.

Figure 5. Filtering compared with stating the disability

Filtering alone was not enough. The filter on its own scored lower than stating the disability, for all three travellers. Using the filter and stating the disability together beat the filter alone for all three, and beat stating alone for the wheelchair user.

Take-home message

Check essential access with clear rules first. Then let ChatGPT choose, using accurate facility data.

Who benefits

  • Travellers with disabilities visiting Saudi Arabia, who get suggestions they can actually use, without losing the activities they came for.
  • Travel platforms that suggest places with AI, who can add our check before the AI chooses. Our code is public, so it can be tested today.
  • Places and tourism bodies, since the narrowing was strongest when facility information was missing.

What we can't claim yet

We used fictional places, one model, one city and one language. Our rules still need to be checked with travellers with disabilities.
